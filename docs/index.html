Bering Strait: Pacific water flowing into the Arctic

More and warmer Pacific water now flows north through the strait, about twice the heat of 1991, and the years with the most heat are the years the Chukchi Sea freezes latest. What we found ↓

What we found

The question: how has the flow through the Bering Strait been affected by the melting of the sea ice? The answer turned the question around: the ice has not changed the flow; the flow, through the heat it carries, changes the ice.

1. More water, and warmer. Measured at the moorings, the yearly flow rose 29% from 1991 to 2024 (0.85 to 1.09 Sv), the water warmed by about 0.8 °C, and the heat carried north about doubled (5 to 11 TW). November sea ice in this map area fell by about two thirds.

2. Heat in, ice out. Year by year, the heat coming through from June to November matches the ice in November closely (r = −0.86 over 28 years; −0.82 with both trends removed): the years with the most heat, 2017 to 2019, froze latest. Heat alone matches about two thirds of the differences between years.

3. The arrow points from water to ice. Published studies find that losing the ice has not changed the flow, which is driven by the ocean and atmosphere far from the strait (Margevich, Timmermans and Danielson 2024; Peralta-Ferriz and Woodgate 2023), while the heat through the strait times the spring melt and the autumn freeze-up (Serreze and others 2016).

Takeaway: the Bering Strait is not a victim of the melting ice; it is one of the reasons the ice is melting. As the climate warms, the Arctic is being warmed from the south, through a gap 82 km wide and 50 m deep. This page does not add new science; it shows the published picture holds when the open measurements are put side by side.

About this page

A recreation from open data, not research: everything that moves or is measured on this page comes from a named dataset. Currents and water temperature: HYCOM GOFS 3.1 (HYCOM consortium), reanalysis GLBv0.08 for 1995 to 2015 and analysis GLBy0.08 for 2019, 2020 and 2023. Measured flow: Bering Strait moorings, R. Woodgate, University of Washington (Woodgate, Stafford and Prahl 2015; Woodgate 2018). Sea ice: NOAA/NSIDC Climate Data Record of passive microwave sea ice concentration, version 6. Seabed and land: IBCAO 5.2. Inset map: Natural Earth. The particles are drawn by this page from the model currents (5 to 8 day tracks); they are not observed drifters.
